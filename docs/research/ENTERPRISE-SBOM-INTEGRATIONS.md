# Enterprise SBOM integration priorities

Research date: 2026-10-10. Tracking: `census-6dt.7`; Dependency-Track compatibility spike: `census-6dt.5`.

Crypto Census should finish the selected Dependency-Track compatibility investigation, then prioritize **Sonatype SBOM Manager and JFrog Artifactory/Xray** for enterprise delivery. Investigate **Cybeats SBOM Studio** alongside them when the customer needs searchable cryptographic inventory. Publish clear GitHub and GitLab CI artifact recipes immediately. Treat Black Duck, Anchore, and Snyk as customer-driven integrations until their handling of our evidence is demonstrated.

This is a recommended integration order, not a market-share ranking. Named customer cases and company filings establish enterprise use; they do not establish how often a prospective Census customer uses a particular SBOM feature. No comparable, independently measured installed-base dataset was found in this research. Product-family adoption is explicitly separated from adoption of an individual module.

The central compatibility question is whether a destination preserves and exposes **our externally generated CBOM**, including `cryptographic-asset`, `cryptoProperties`, occurrences, Census risk properties, health counters, and provenance. CycloneDX import, SPDX import, a native cryptography scanner, and CBOM export each describe different capabilities. None alone proves that external Census evidence survives import.

Do not build a Census CBOM adapter under the current contract. [Enterprise cases](https://www.mend.io/customer-success-stories/), [explicit limitation](https://docs.mend.io/platform/latest/update-a-project-using-an-sbom-import).
- **FOSSA:** has genuine external SBOM import, including a signed-upload workflow. That is stronger evidence than export alone, but no complete Census CBOM retention contract was established. Revisit with customer demand. [Import API](https://docs.fossa.com/docs/sbom/importing-sboms).
- **Finite State:** a credible product-security option with external SBOM ingestion and native binary cryptography discovery. The latter does not prove preservation of an externally generated Census CBOM. More relevant to embedded/product-security customers than the initial Maven workflow; request a current import schema before promoting it. [Platform datasheet](https://finitestate.io/hubfs/Collateral/Finite%20State%20Next%20GenerationMDM%20Datasheet.pdf).
- **PQCA CBOMkit:** promising reference compatibility target. It explicitly imports external CBOMs through `POST /api/v1/cbom/{projectIdentifier}` and exposes stored CBOM queries. Treat it as a cryptographic inventory interoperability target; this research does not establish broad enterprise deployment. IBM origins should not obscure its current PQCA context. [Architecture](https://pqca.org/blog/2026/pqca-cbomkit-architecture/).
- **IBM Guardium Quantum Safe:** elevate to specialist discovery alongside Cybeats when enterprise cryptographic posture management is the requirement. IBM documents CBOM JSON ingestion through built-in plugins using SFTP, REST, or object storage; this is stronger evidence than native discovery alone. Exact acceptance of Census fields, retrieval, service authentication, and asynchronous completion still need verification against the customer's product/version. ## Release gate for each adapter

Use one versioned capability fixture containing real Census output plus controlled crypto assets, multiple occurrences, custom risk properties, nonzero health counters, and provenance. Also exercise an actual Maven-produced merged CBOM. Keep the source SHA-256 and never alter the evidence to make a receiver accept it.

1. Submit exact bytes to a dedicated project/version; record the destination version, auth permission scope, request identity, HTTP result, and asynchronous job identifier.
2. Confirm durable ingestion through the vendor's supported completion API. Exercise rejected format, wrong project, insufficient permissions, rate limiting, failed background processing, timeout, and duplicate submission. A `2xx` or an inactive token alone is insufficient.
3. Retrieve the original and compare SHA-256. Separately export/query the derived inventory and compare component type, `cryptoProperties`, occurrence locations, Census risk properties, health, and provenance. Record retained, normalized, dropped, rejected, and unqueryable fields individually.
4. Test retrieval after restart and document retention, deletion, project-version overwrite, permissions, private CA/proxy support, and artifact limits. Do not claim exactly-once delivery without a receiver contract proving it.
5. Publish two independent capabilities: **original evidence retained** and **cryptographic inventory preserved/queryable**. A destination may support either or both. If its derived inventory loses information, any supported integration must make that loss explicit and preserve a separately retrievable original; that policy requires a deliberate product decision.

For Dependency-Track specifically, Census custom properties may retain primitive, mode, padding, or key-size information even if standard `cryptoProperties` fields disappear. Report standard-field loss, actual semantic loss, and queryability separately; duplicated custom properties must not be mistaken for native CBOM support.

The current research is documentation and source review. No commercial tenant was contacted or written to, and no completed live vendor round trip is claimed. Dependency-Track's isolated local round trip is the next empirical step. Subsequent adapter work should follow customer demand and verified fidelity, rather than expanding every vendor named here.

## Combining runtime cryptography evidence with a software SBOM

**Recommendation: reuse the customer's existing software SBOM generator and add an explicit enrichment step.** The generator owns package inventory, versions, licenses, and the software dependency graph. Census owns cryptographic observations, occurrence evidence, assessment policy, and collection health. The enrichment step consumes both artifacts from the same build, produces a derived view, and retains both originals and their hashes. This is a proposed architecture: Census does not currently implement this cross-BOM join or package ownership resolution.

CycloneDX can represent software and cryptographic assets together. Its cryptographic model includes algorithms, protocols, keys, and related material. That gives us a standard representation for observed cryptography without taking responsibility for enumerating every software package. [CycloneDX CBOM capability](https://cyclonedx.org/capabilities/cbom/).

| Representation | Suitable when | Required behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **One enriched CycloneDX BOM** | A destination expects one authoritative inventory per application/version and supports the required crypto fields. | Copy the software inventory and graph, add Census crypto assets and supported relationships, preserve provenance, and emit a new derived artifact. Validate against an explicit compatible CycloneDX schema version. Keep both originals; the derived BOM has its own identity and hash. |
| **Separate SBOM and CBOM, connected with BOM-Link** | Independent producers, update schedules, access controls, or preservation of signed originals matter, and the consumer can resolve the links. | Keep each BOM immutable; use stable serial number/version identities and supported cross-document references. Retain a manifest locating and hashing both files. Test consumer resolution and authorization explicitly. |

BOM-Link identifies a whole BOM or a specific object using `urn:cdx:<uuid>/<version>#<bom-ref>`; the fragment is optional. It is an identifier, not an automatic network retrieval protocol. A storage/resolution contract is still required. If an existing SBOM lacks a serial number, do not silently modify a signed original to invent one: produce a clearly derived document or use a manifest until the producer supplies a stable identity. [BOM-Link specification](https://cyclonedx.org/capabilities/bomlink/).

The join must be evidence-based:

- **Anchor the build first.** Require matching application/module, release/build identity, source revision where available, and input artifact hashes. PURL plus exact version/qualifiers identifies a package candidate; a digest of the actual runtime artifact provides stronger confirmation of the bytes observed. A build ID alone does not prove that the running class came from a particular dependency. CycloneDX supports package identifiers, artifact hashes, and identity evidence. [Software component identity](https://www.cyclonedx.org/use-cases/software-components/).
- **Resolve the observing caller separately from the provider.** A callsite may establish that application code requested RSA; it does not by itself prove which dependency or native provider implemented RSA. Future class-to-artifact attribution must handle shaded JARs, relocated packages, duplicate classes, JDK modules, and native providers. Keep ambiguous ownership unresolved and retain the occurrence; do not guess from class/package names or invent an algorithm PURL.
- **Use the correct relationship.** A verified consumer can depend on a cryptographic asset; a verified implementation can `provide` it. These are different claims, and a library implementing an algorithm does not establish that the algorithm ran. Keep the observed callsite alongside the relationship. [CycloneDX implementation relationships](https://cyclonedx.org/use-cases/implementation-details/).
- **Rewrite references safely.** Namespace colliding `bom-ref` values using a recorded source-BOM mapping and rewrite every affected reference throughout the document, not only the dependency list. Preserve distinctions between software nodes and crypto nodes; do not deduplicate solely by algorithm name. Validate uniqueness and reference integrity before publishing. CycloneDX defines document-local reference uniqueness, dependency relationships, and scoped completeness. [Pinned CycloneDX 1.6 schema](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CycloneDX/specification/1.6/schema/bom-1.6.schema.json).
- **Keep provenance and coverage attached to the evidence.** Record input hashes, producer versions, run identity, join rules, ambiguous/unmatched counts, policy version, and the original health counters. Do not reuse a source signature as a signature over transformed content. Runtime observation proves exercised behavior during the collection window; absence is unknown, not evidence that a dependency uses no cryptography or is quantum safe. An otherwise complete software SBOM must not promote the crypto subset to complete. Use scoped completeness declarations where appropriate and retain the more specific Census collection-health evidence.

Destination upload semantics are part of this architecture. **Do not upload the ordinary SBOM and then the CBOM to the same application/version assuming the receiver adds their contents.** Some inventory APIs treat each upload as a replacement snapshot; additive APIs may instead accumulate stale components. The adapter's versioned capability test must upload an SBOM with package A, then a CBOM with crypto B, and inspect whether A remains, B appears, and removals behave as documented. Dependency-Track's empirical spike is investigating this contract; no result is assumed here.

For a replacement receiver, publish one validated enriched snapshot after both producers finish, or use separately identified inventories with a verified linking strategy. For an additive receiver, require explicit ownership/removal semantics before enabling repeated updates. In either case, retain the source SBOM, source CBOM, derived artifact if present, join manifest, and delivery receipt. Enrichment also needs an explicit validation contract: the current publisher's strict Census-CBOM boundary must not be assumed to accept an arbitrary mixed software/crypto document.
